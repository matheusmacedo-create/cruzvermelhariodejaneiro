The Tim Maia cycle path was closed at 1.07am on Wednesday 2 September on the stretch between São Conrado beach and the Vidigal metal footbridge, because of the heavy surf hitting Rio's seafront. The information comes from Rio City Hall's Operations and Resilience Centre (COR-Rio).

COR-Rio coordinates the monitoring of, and response to, events that affect mobility and urban safety. The Brazilian Red Cross — Rio de Janeiro Branch plays a complementary role, focused on risk reduction, community preparedness and humanitarian support in emergencies, working alongside the public authorities.

The closure matters because it protects cyclists and pedestrians on a stretch exposed to strong waves. According to COR-Rio, Avenida Niemeyer was operating normally between São Conrado and Leblon, keeping an essential daily route open while the sea conditions lasted.

## What changed for traffic and on the seafront

Rio City Hall said the closure of the cycle path did not affect Avenida Niemeyer, which remained open. According to the Brazilian Navy, waves of 2.5 to 3 metres could reach the seafront from Wednesday morning until 9am on Thursday 3 September.

The Tim Maia cycle path is closed only when stronger sea and weather conditions are confirmed. The criteria for a closure include:

- waves above 2 metres high at intervals of up to 15 seconds;
- winds above 65 km/h at the Vidigal weather station;
- heavy rain in the area.

## How the closure and the warning work

When a closure is ordered, users are warned by sound alarms and signs on site. The operation includes fixed and illuminated message panels with an audible alert, closing of the barriers and more municipal guards along the route.

Cyclists and pedestrians are asked to respect the physical barriers and the signs, and not to use the closed stretch. The aim is to reduce the risk of accidents caused by waves that may reach the path.

## Recommendations during heavy surf

- Avoid swimming and water sports where there is heavy surf.
- Do not stay at seafront viewpoints or close to the sea while the warning is in force.
- Beachgoers should strictly follow the instructions of the Fire Brigade teams.
- Fishers should avoid going out to sea while the heavy surf lasts.
- Avoid cycling along the seafront if the waves are reaching the cycle path.
- In the event of an accident at sea, do not try to rescue anyone yourself. Call the Fire Brigade immediately on 193.
