The Brazilian Red Cross — Rio de Janeiro Branch welcomed representatives of the Rio de Janeiro State Civil Firefighters Association (Associação de Bombeiros Civis do Estado do Rio) to its headquarters, to strengthen ties and discuss future cooperation. The meeting brought together the branch's vice-president, Antônio Pedregal, its secretary-general, Paulo Simão, and the board of the partner organisation.

The meeting was institutional in nature and marked the start of a formal relationship between two organisations that each work, in their own way, on the front line of emergency response in the state. The discussion centred on possible areas of joint work and on mutual recognition of each organisation's capabilities.

## Who the organisations are

The Brazilian Red Cross is part of the International Red Cross and Red Crescent Movement, the largest humanitarian network in the world, guided by seven fundamental principles — among them humanity, impartiality, neutrality and independence. In Rio de Janeiro, the branch works in assistance to vulnerable populations, support in emergencies and disasters, and the training of volunteers and professionals in first aid.

Civil firefighters, for their part, are qualified professionals who work in fire prevention and firefighting, rescue and pre-hospital care in private settings: residential buildings, shopping centres, hospitals, industry, events and large developments. Organised in professional associations, such as the Rio de Janeiro State Civil Firefighters Association, these workers complement the public emergency services and are often the first to arrive when an accident happens inside these places.

## Why closer ties matter

Rio de Janeiro faces recurring risks: heavy rain, landslides, flooding, fires in densely built-up areas and large crowds at events and popular festivals. In all of these contexts, the difference between a serious outcome and a successful response often lies in the first few minutes — and in who is prepared to act.

Bringing a humanitarian organisation closer to a professional group specialised in emergencies widens the response capacity of both. In practice, partnerships like this tend to lead to technical exchange, joint training, mutual support in event cover and better coordination when an incident requires more than one team at the same site.

## What may come next

The meeting did not settle specific actions, but it opened a channel of dialogue between the two boards. The expectation is that the talks will move towards concrete forms of cooperation, always with the same aim stated by both sides: to benefit the people of Rio de Janeiro State.

For the branch, working with technical organisations reinforces a long-standing part of its mission — spreading life-saving knowledge. The branch runs a permanent programme of [in-person courses](/en/courses/), taught in Portuguese, for professionals and for members of the community who want to learn to help safely.

## Follow the next steps

The next steps of this and other partnerships are published in the branch's [news section](/en/news/), where institutional activities, campaigns and calls for volunteers are announced.

The branch's humanitarian work also depends on the support of society. You can contribute through the official [donation channels](/en/donate/), which sustain assistance, training and emergency response across the state.
