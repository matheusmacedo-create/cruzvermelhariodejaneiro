Brazilian Red Cross — Rio de Janeiro Branch

Part of the International Red Cross and Red Crescent Movement, serving the state of Rio de Janeiro.

Who we are

The Brazilian Red Cross — Rio de Janeiro Branch is the Rio de Janeiro State branch of the Brazilian Red Cross, the National Society of the International Red Cross and Red Crescent Movement in Brazil. The Movement brings together the International Committee of the Red Cross, the International Federation of Red Cross and Red Crescent Societies and the National Society of each country, all bound by the same seven fundamental principles.

We are a non-profit organisation, registered under Brazilian tax ID (CNPJ) 08.560.973/0001-97, and recognised as being of municipal public utility by Municipal Law 5,153/2010 and of state public utility by State Law 9,984/2023. The work is led by an executive board and thematic coordination teams, and carried out largely by volunteers trained by the branch itself.

What we do

We train volunteers, teach in-person first-aid and vocational courses at our headquarters, run preventive education and support communities across the state through campaigns such as the Winter Clothing Drive and the Impacto das Cores project.

Our training arm is the Escola de Educação e Saúde CVB-RJ, which offers seven in-person courses: Basic First Aid, First Aid under the Lucas Law (for settings with children), Basic Life Support, Venipuncture, Civil Firefighter, Carer for Older People and Lip Micropigmentation. Classes are taught in Portuguese, in person, in downtown Rio de Janeiro.

Our headquarters

The branch operates from the Red Cross Palace, at Praça da Cruz Vermelha, 10, in the centre of Rio de Janeiro. The building is the historic headquarters of the Brazilian Red Cross and was granted provisional heritage protection by IPHAN, Brazil's National Historic and Artistic Heritage Institute, in 2021.

The branch does not run a clinic, schedule medical appointments or respond to emergencies. In an emergency in Brazil, call 192 (ambulance) or 193 (fire brigade).

The Movement we belong to

The International Red Cross and Red Crescent Movement is the largest humanitarian network in the world, and it has three parts. The International Committee of the Red Cross works in armed conflict, with a mandate given to it by the Geneva Conventions. The International Federation of Red Cross and Red Crescent Societies coordinates the response to disasters outside conflict. And in each country there is one National Society, auxiliary to the public authorities in humanitarian matters — in Brazil, the Brazilian Red Cross.

This branch is the Rio de Janeiro State arm of that National Society. The emblem is not a brand: it is a protective sign defined in international humanitarian law, and its use is restricted. That is also why the seven fundamental principles below are not a statement of values but the conditions under which the Movement is allowed to work — neutrality and impartiality are what let a Red Cross team reach people that no one else can reach.

What the branch runs

Five fronts, all carried out from the headquarters in central Rio de Janeiro and across the state.

Volunteer training

The branch trains its own volunteers, with no requirement of a health background. After registering, each volunteer goes through initial training at the headquarters and then joins one of the coordination teams — humanitarian action, first aid, disaster risk management, youth, sport, psychosocial support.

First-aid training

In-person courses in basic first aid, first aid for settings with children under the Lucas Law, and Basic Life Support, open to anyone. This is also what the branch brings to events and field operations.

Impacto das Cores

Collective painting efforts that bring community spaces back into use. The point is as much the work itself as the bond it builds between volunteers and residents.

Winter Clothing Drive

Collection of coats, blankets and shoes for families in vulnerable situations, received at the headquarters from Monday to Friday, 10am to 5pm, and sorted before distribution.

Training for companies and schools

Occupational-safety (NR) training, first-aid training under the Lucas Law and programmes built around a particular team. It is one of the main ways partner organisations support the branch.

The seven fundamental principles

  • HumanityTo prevent and alleviate human suffering wherever it may be found, protecting life and dignity.
  • ImpartialityTo make no discrimination as to nationality, race, religion, class or political opinion; need alone sets priority.
  • NeutralityTo take no side in hostilities or in controversies of a political, racial, religious or ideological nature.
  • IndependenceTo be auxiliary to the public authorities while keeping the autonomy to act always in line with the Movement's principles.
  • Voluntary serviceRelief given voluntarily and without any desire for gain.
  • UnityOne National Society in each country, open to all and present throughout its territory.
  • UniversalityAll National Societies have equal status and share equal responsibilities in helping each other.
